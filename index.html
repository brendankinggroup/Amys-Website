The method

Classical foundation. Contemporary practice.

Amy trained in the classical repertoire in New York and learned the athletic West Coast approach in Las Vegas. Pilates High is the place where the two meet: precise, progressive, and never gentle for the sake of it.

The practice

Fifty-five minutes. One client. Full apparatus.

Group classes teach the exercise. Private training teaches you the exercise: the hip that sits a little higher, the shoulder that wants to do the work your abdominals should be doing, the breath you hold on the way down. That is what the hour is for.

An assessment before anything else
The first visit is ninety minutes. Posture, gait, range of motion, and a conversation about what your body needs to do on a Tuesday afternoon. The programme comes from that, not from a template.
Written, then rewritten
You leave every session with a record of what we did and what changed. The next session starts from there. Nothing is repeated because it is convenient.
Traditional to hybrid, by design
Some bodies need the slow, classical order. Some need spring-loaded athletic work that leaves you shaking. Most need both, in a ratio that shifts as you get stronger. Amy writes that ratio into every programme.

“In ten sessions you will feel the difference. In twenty you will see the difference. In thirty you will have a whole new body.”

Joseph Pilates, Return to Life Through Contrology, 1945
Who it's for

Bodies with a specific job to do.

Private training is the right fit when general fitness is not the goal. Most clients arrive with one of these.

  • Rebuilding after injury or surgery

    Post-rehab work that picks up where physical therapy discharges you, with spinal, hip, and shoulder protocols written in coordination with your clinician.

  • Rotational sport

    Golf, tennis, and pickleball ask the spine to rotate under load. We train the rotation to come from the right place, so power goes up and the low back stays quiet.

  • Pre- and postnatal

    Pelvic floor, deep abdominal reconnection, and load management through each trimester and the year after.

  • Strength and bone after fifty

    Progressive spring resistance for bone density, balance, and the hip and spinal extension that keeps you upright in your eighties.

  • Hypermobility and scoliosis

    Bodies that need stability more than stretch. Spring-loaded work that teaches the joints where to stop, and asymmetric programming that meets the curve where it is.

  • The desk-bound spine

    Long hours seated shorten the front of the body and switch off the back. We reverse it, one session at a time, with homework that takes six minutes.

The apparatus

Balanced Body, the full studio, all of it yours for the hour.

Balanced Body builds the apparatus most serious studios choose: maple frames, precise spring gauges, and the small adjustments that let a session be tuned to one person. There is no waiting for a machine here. There is one client, and every piece is set for them.

Reformer with TowerThe sliding carriage and spring system that teaches control through resistance and instability. The Tower adds the roll-down bar, push-through bar, and leg springs, so most of the repertoire lives here.
Trapeze TableSprings from above and below for assisted spinal articulation, hanging work, and the exercises that are too demanding for the mat.
Combo ChairA split pedal on springs. Unforgiving, precise, and the fastest route to functional leg, hip, and standing strength.
Ladder BarrelSpinal extension, hip flexor length, and the stretching most bodies have been avoiding for years.
Arc and Spine CorrectorCurved supports that restore the natural curves of the spine and open the chest, one vertebra at a time.
