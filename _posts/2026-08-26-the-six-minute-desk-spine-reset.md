A long day at a desk does two predictable things. It shortens the front of the body, the hip flexors and the chest, and it switches off the back, the glutes and the muscles between the shoulder blades. Six minutes a day will not undo eight hours, but it interrupts the pattern before it sets.

Do these in order. Slow counts. Breathe out on the effort.

<!--more-->

## 1. Standing roll-down, four repetitions

Feet hip-width, knees soft. Nod the chin and peel the spine forward one vertebra at a time until the hands hang. Breathe there for two breaths. Roll back up the same way, stacking from the base. This is the mat version of what the Cadillac roll-down bar does with a spring.

## 2. Hip flexor kneel, one minute each side

One knee on the floor, the other foot forward. Tuck the pelvis slightly and shift forward until you feel the front of the back hip. Do not lunge deeper. Hold, breathe, and keep the ribs over the pelvis.

## 3. Prone extension, eight repetitions

Lie face down, forehead resting on the backs of the hands. On an exhale, lift the head, hands and chest an inch, lengthening the crown of the head away from the feet. Lower slowly. This is the muscle group a chair spends all day telling to relax.

## 4. Chest opener on the wall, one minute

Stand with your back against a wall, heels a hand's width away. Bring the arms to a goalpost shape and slide them up the wall as far as the back of the hands will stay in contact. Down and up, slowly.

That is it. Set a timer for the end of the workday and do it before you leave the room. Consistency beats intensity every single time, on the mat and on the apparatus.
