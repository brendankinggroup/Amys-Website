Direction

Fitness and calm are not opposites here. They are the same room at two times of day.

Think of a Balanced Body studio at seven in the morning: maple, springs, linen upholstery, and a long slant of desert light across the floor. Nothing on the page is louder than that light. The strength shows in the work, not in the graphics.

Three references, for feel rather than copying: the restraint of an Aesop store, the editorial confidence of a fashion magazine's opening spread, and the quiet of a good physiotherapy clinic. High end reads as fewer things, placed carefully, with room around them.

Palette
Stone#EEEAE3
Linen#E4DFD6
Eucalyptus#5F6E5F
Moss#252C27
Ink#1F211E

Warm stone and linen for the ground, so photographs of wood and skin sit naturally on it. Eucalyptus is the only colour and it appears once per screen. Moss carries the pricing so the numbers feel considered rather than commercial.

Type

Bodoni Moda, headlines

Strength, quietly built.

A high-contrast Didone, the face fashion magazines use for a reason. Set large, with an italic word where the sentence turns.

Instrument Sans, everything else

A quiet grotesque for body text, labels, and buttons. It never competes with the headline and it stays legible at the small tracked-out sizes the labels use.

Imagery

Until the shoot happens, the site draws its own imagery: fine line drawings of the apparatus over painted washes of light. They are placeholders with a point of view, and they will still hold the page after real photographs arrive.

The shot list

Six photographs would carry the whole site. One half-day with a photographer, natural light only, no flash, no posed smiling at camera. Shoot in colour; the edit should be warm and slightly desaturated, never black and white.

  • Hero

    Portrait orientation. A client mid-exercise on the Reformer, carriage extended, shot from a low angle at the foot end so the springs and footbar lead the eye. Window light from one side. Face turned away or cropped; the body is the subject.

    Reference search on Unsplash

  • The empty studio

    Wide, landscape. Apparatus in a row, no people, early light across the floor. This is the calm frame. It sits full width behind the line "Classical foundation. Contemporary practice."

    Reference search on Unsplash

  • Amy, portrait

    Portrait orientation, three-quarter length, standing beside the Reformer with one hand resting on the footbar. Neutral clothing, no logo. Direct, calm, not smiling for the camera. This is the most important photograph on the site.

  • Hands on springs

    Close detail. Amy's hands changing a spring on the Reformer. Shallow depth of field. Says "adjusted for you" without a word of copy.

  • Correction

    Amy's hand on a client's shoulder blade or hip during an exercise. The one image that shows what private training actually is.

  • Texture

    Maple, linen upholstery, and a spring coil, close enough to be abstract. Used small, as a pause between sections.
